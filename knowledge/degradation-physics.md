# Degradation diagnostics and physics-informed AI

## Degradation diagnostics

Common diagnostic inputs include voltage-capacity curves, incremental capacity (ICA), differential voltage (DVA), EIS, relaxation voltage, resistance/DCIR, temperature, force/swelling, and multimodal electrical-thermal-mechanical measurements.

LLI and LAM are degradation modes/descriptors inferred through suitable diagnostics or models. Lithium plating, SEI growth, electrolyte loss, particle cracking, and interfacial degradation are mechanisms/processes that require stronger evidence.

Do not jump from `the model attends to 3.6–3.8 V` to `this proves lithium plating`. A defensible argument connects the signal region to a known diagnostic feature and ideally independent electrochemical/model-based evidence.

For ICA/DVA, report smoothing/interpolation/differentiation choices when they materially affect peaks. Temperature and C-rate can shift diagnostic features.

For EIS, interpret frequency regions cautiously unless equivalent-circuit/electrochemical analysis supports assignment to ohmic, charge-transfer, interfacial, or diffusion processes.

## Physics-informed AI

Possible physics sources include governing equations, ECM states/parameters, SPM/SPMe/DFN outputs, conservation relations, thermodynamic bounds, monotonic degradation priors, parameter bounds, physically meaningful latent variables, or simulated data generated by a calibrated physical model.

Physics may enter through inputs, architecture/state transitions, latent variables, a loss/residual, training data, inference constraints, or post-processing.

A model is not physics-informed merely because voltage/current/temperature are inputs or predictions are smoothed. If physics only motivates feature selection, `physics-guided`, `physics-enhanced`, or `hybrid` may be more accurate.

Compare against a data-only counterpart and validate the claimed physical benefit using constraint violations, monotonicity, parameter plausibility, extrapolation, sparse-data behavior, cross-domain stability, or interpretable physical states.

If simulation data are used, distinguish them clearly from experiments and describe model calibration, domain mismatch, and transfer/adaptation.
